ingredients:
- 1/4 cup whole-wheat flour
- 3 cups King Arthur all-purpose flour (or bread flour)
- 1 1/2 tsp table salt
- 1 tsp instant or rapid-rise yeast
- 1 1/2 cups water, room temperature
- Cornmeal or semolina flour, for dusting
- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- 1 Tbsp chopped fresh rosemary, divided
- 2 tsp coarse sea salt, divided

directions:
- Sift whole-wheat flour through fine-mesh strainer into bowl of stand mixer; discard bran. Add all-purpose flour, salt, and yeast. Using dough hook on low speed, slowly add water and mix until cohesive dough starts to form and no dry flour remains, 5 to 7 minutes, scraping down bowl as needed. Transfer dough to lightly oiled large bowl, cover tightly with plastic wrap, and let rise for 30 minutes.
- Fold the dough — holding edge of dough with fingertips, fold edge toward itself by gently lifting and folding over; repeat, turning bowl and folding 8 times total. Cover and let rise 30 minutes. Repeat folding and rising every 30 minutes, 3 more times. After fourth set of folds, cover bowl tightly with plastic and refrigerate for at least 16 hours or up to 2 days.
- Transfer dough to lightly floured counter, stretch gently into 8-inch round (do not deflate), and divide in half. Gently stretch and fold each half to create a rough triangle with 5-inch sides. Transfer seam side down to lightly oiled baking sheet, cover loosely with oiled plastic, and rest until no longer cold to the touch, 30 minutes to 1 hour.
- Place baking stone on lower-middle rack and heat oven to 450°F. Line 2 overturned rimmed baking sheets with parchment and dust liberally with cornmeal. Roll 1 piece of dough into triangular shape with 8-inch base and 10-inch sides, about 1/2 inch thick. Transfer to prepared sheet.
- Using a pizza cutter, make a 6-inch-long cut down center of triangle, through dough to sheet, leaving about 1/2 inch at either end. Make three 2- to 3-inch diagonal cuts through dough on each side of center cut, leaving 1-inch border to create a leaf-vein pattern. Gently stretch dough toward sides of sheet to widen cuts; overall leaf should measure about 10 by 12 inches. Cover loosely with oiled plastic and let rise until nearly doubled, 30 to 45 minutes. Twenty minutes after shaping first loaf, repeat rolling, cutting, and shaping with second piece of dough.
- Brush top and sides of first loaf with 2 tablespoons oil. Sprinkle evenly with 1 1/2 teaspoons rosemary and 1 teaspoon sea salt. Slide parchment with loaf onto baking stone and bake until golden brown, 18 to 22 minutes, rotating halfway through. Transfer to wire rack, discard parchment, and let cool 20 minutes. Repeat topping and baking with second loaf. Serve warm or at room temperature.

---

Best eaten within 4 hours of baking. Variations: Asiago and black pepper (omit rosemary and sea salt, top each loaf with 1 tsp coarse pepper and 1/2 cup grated Asiago); Bacon and Gruyère (cook 4 slices thick-cut bacon, add to dough, top with 1/2 cup shredded Gruyère per loaf); Olive (add 1 cup chopped kalamata olives to dough).
